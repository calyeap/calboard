Step 2 — Fact acquisition and spot-check

Human · blocks all calculation

Before anything is calculated, you check each number against the document it came from — one at a time, with no way to approve them in bulk.

Why it matters
Every figure in the final report is built on these. A wrong input here does not produce an obviously wrong answer downstream; it produces a plausible one, which is worse. This step is the only place the error is cheap to catch.
What you are deciding
Confirm means you found this figure in the source and it matches. Cannot verify means you could not — for any reason. There is deliberately no third option and no box to type a corrected figure into.
What to examine
The cards are ordered by risk, not by importance: figures a model read out of a filing come first, then figures from data providers, then unverified ones, then machine-tagged primary data last.
Why can't I just correct a wrong number?
Because a typed figure would have no source, no source class and no extraction type — and every number in this system is required to carry all three to its point of display. A hand-entered value would be a fact with no provenance, which is the exact failure this step exists to prevent. If a figure is wrong, the run stops as INCOMPLETE and the fact is re-acquired from a source. This is stricter than most analysts expect: one bad feed value stalls the whole run.

Every material fact is confirmed individually against its source. There is no bulk control. No calculation module can run until the last fact carries a decision — enforced server-side, not by the router. Queue order is by risk: AI-extracted first, then secondary, then unverified, then structured primary.

Judgments — labelled FACT, and not facts

Select, do not type

Three inputs look like facts and are actually choices. You make them here, in the open, rather than having the software quietly pick one.

Why it matters
Where a company has restated its accounts, there is more than one defensible way to measure a ten-year record — and they give different answers. Any of them is fine once it is labelled. Mixing them silently is not, because the resulting figure describes no real accounting basis at all.
What to examine
The size of the gap between the options. Where two defensible choices produce nearly the same figure, the choice barely matters. Where they diverge, that difference travels into every number downstream.
