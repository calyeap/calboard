## Objective and style

Maximise **long-term investment returns with meaningful risk control**. Risk control focuses on permanent impairment, concentration, poor sizing of uncertain ideas, leverage, behavioural mistakes and process failures. Normal volatility is acceptable.

Portfolio style: moderately concentrated, growth-leaning, quality-conscious, valuation-disciplined, long-term, low-to-moderate activity and hands-off by default.

Hold while **the investment thesis remains intact and forward expected return remains attractive**.

## Core principles

- Quality and valuation both matter.
- Forward expected return matters more than cost basis.
- Price movement triggers analysis; it does not determine the conclusion.
- Conviction does not cancel uncertainty.
- Avoid permanent impairment.
- Analysis depth increases with **position size + uncertainty**.
- Growth never overrides valuation discipline.
- Use the minimum modelling and workflow complexity necessary to make the decision well.
- Provenance is necessary but does not by itself prove correctness.
- Cross-check agreement does not prove correctness when models or sources share the same assumptions or origin.
- CalFinance and Calboard should assist judgement without manufacturing certainty.

## Investment thesis and business quality

A purchase must clear minimum bars for:

1. business quality;
2. valuation / forward expected return.

The thesis should make the expectations difference explicit where practical:

**what the market appears to require → what Calvin expects instead → why the evidence supports that difference → what would invalidate it.**

A catalyst is optional. A testable difference in expectations is not.

Preferred business characteristics include attractive reinvestment, durable growth runway, improving economics, credible management, manageable leverage, profitability / FCF or a credible path to strong cash generation.

Moat claims should explain the **causal economic mechanism** that may persist — for example switching costs, pricing power, cost advantage, network effects, scarcity, brand / intangible advantage or another defensible mechanism. High margins or ROIC can support the case but do not prove a moat by themselves.

Management quality includes operating execution, capital allocation, acquisitions, reinvestment, financing and dilution. The relevant question is not only whether management grows the business, but whether it creates durable **value per share**.

## Research and evidence standards

Material factual claims should preferentially trace to primary / original evidence where practical.

For material conclusions, preserve the chain:

**claim → supporting evidence → source / fact → relevant calculation or transformation → interpretation.**

Corroboration is judged by **independence, relevance and quality**, not source count. Several articles repeating one management statement are not several independent confirmations. Several models using the same assumption are not independent validation.

Normalization, model outputs and AI synthesis must remain reviewable. A well-sourced number can still be mapped, transformed or interpreted incorrectly.

The strongest credible counterargument and materially contrary evidence should be surfaced rather than excluded because they weaken the thesis.

## Valuation methodology

Valuation uses a **blended methodology**: understand scenarios, use the method appropriate to the business, and cross-check where practical. Avoid false precision.

No single diagnostic, reverse valuation, multiple, DCF or heuristic is allowed to become the investment verdict.

### Scenario coherence

Scenario growth, margins, reinvestment, working capital, financing, dilution and ownership should describe the **same economic outcome** when material.

Growth should not appear without the reinvestment, funding or ownership consequences required to support it.

Use the minimum detail needed to achieve economic consistency. A full three-statement model is **not** required by default.

### Cash-flow / discount-rate consistency

Cash flow and discount rate must correspond to the same claimant.

- Firm cash flow requires a firm-consistent discount rate.
- Equity cash flow requires an equity-consistent discount rate.

Low leverage may make an approximation acceptable in some cases, but it does **not** make firm and equity bases theoretically identical. Any approximation must remain within a validated domain and tolerance before it becomes governing practice.

### Reverse valuation

Reverse valuation is a core diagnostic: ask what the current market price requires rather than relying only on an analyst-authored fair value.

The solve remains conditional on the chosen model shape, cash-flow definition and assumptions. It shows what the specified model requires; it does not reveal the market's literal forecast with certainty.

### Price-implied success weight

Where two assumed outcome values are used, preserve the interpolation calculation only as a **conditional price-implied break-even success weight**.

It must not be presented as an identified real-world probability of success.

### Solver and model-limit states

A failed, unstable, non-unique or uninformative solve establishes a limitation of that model under those assumptions.

It does **not** automatically establish mathematical impossibility, economic impossibility or that the company cannot be valued using another valid approach.

Fail toward saying less when the model does not support a stronger claim.

### Common-equity completeness

Value attributed to common shareholders must treat material economically senior or non-common claims consistently.

Material debt, preferred claims, minority interests, convertibles or other relevant claims must either:

- be incorporated consistently; or
- cause the affected equity valuation to be qualified, constrained or suppressed.

Materiality controls complexity. The methodology does not require exhaustive modelling of immaterial claims.

### Yield + growth

Yield + growth may be used only as a **sanity-check heuristic**, not as a general expected-return formula.

Use requires a credible forward or normalized owner-cash-flow base and economically sustainable growth / reinvestment assumptions.

### Terminal economics

Terminal growth, reinvestment and sustainable returns on capital must be economically consistent.

Persistent excess returns require company-specific economic support. A diagnostic excess-return spread may be used as a labelled analytical convention; the convention itself is not evidence that the company can sustain that spread indefinitely.

### RONIC / incremental returns

Incremental-return analysis remains useful but must be interpreted cautiously where measurement is distorted by acquisitions, investment lags, one-off projects, capital-light economics or changes in business mix.

Do not add complicated normalization merely to make the metric look cleaner. Increase complexity only when the simpler measure cannot answer a material question.

### Scenario uncertainty

Bear / base / bull scenarios are selected analytical cases, not confidence intervals or maximum-loss bounds.

Equal scenario weights may be used as a display / comparison convention. Equal weights do not claim that each scenario has a one-third real-world probability, and their mean is not automatically an estimated expected value.

### Valuation-position semantics

The permitted semantic states are:

- **CHEAP** — evidence supports undervaluation.
- **FAIR** — evidence supports roughly fair valuation.
- **EXPENSIVE** — evidence supports overvaluation.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** — material valuation evidence conflicts or is insufficient to support one of the other states.

**INCONCLUSIVE is distinct from FAIR.**

Numerical valuation-position cut-points remain **TEST / provisional and non-governing** until adequately validated and explicitly approved.

## Cash flow, normalization, tax and ownership

Preserve distinct economic concepts rather than collapsing them into one FCF number.

Cash-flow analysis should consider, when material:

- operating cash generation;
- reinvestment needed to support growth;
- working-capital effects;
- leases and financed capacity;
- stock-based compensation;
- financing;
- taxes;
- ownership dilution.

Normalized cash flow must reconcile back to reported figures and explain each material adjustment. “Normalized” may not become an unexplained add-back, remove investment required to support forecast growth, or hide a real economic cost.

Extend historical windows only when doing so resolves a material uncertainty. A mandatory ten-year quarterly history is not part of the methodology.

Use the tax basis appropriate to the relevant cash flow and company situation. Abnormal tax periods, losses and NOLs should be treated explicitly when material. No fixture tax rate becomes universal policy.

Stock-based compensation, compensation expense, cash generation and dilution are related but distinct. The methodology must avoid both omission and double counting. Exact single-count treatment remains TEST until validated.

## Pre-revenue / high-uncertainty valuation

Pre-revenue and very high-uncertainty businesses should be underwritten through coherent commercial outcomes, scale requirements, funding needs, dilution, financing risk and failure states rather than forcing a mature-company valuation template onto weak evidence.

A conventional DCF may still be mathematically possible, but the evidence may not support treating it as decision-useful.

Funding timing, financing risk and dilution must be consistent with the valuation of the residual equity claim.

The exact simplest practical pre-revenue financing method remains **TEST / provisional** until adequately validated. No unvalidated replacement formula, fixed rate cap or implementation shortcut is governing methodology.

## Macro / geopolitics / technicals

Macro and geopolitics have moderate influence through assumptions, risk and sizing. They normally do not override a strong company-level thesis.

**Do not wait for the world to feel safe before investing.**

Technical analysis has a minor role only: entry timing, staging and unusual price / volume context. It never determines fundamental investability.

## Required process before a new BUY

Require:

1. written investment thesis;
2. valuation / expected-return assessment;
3. key risks;
4. thesis invalidation criteria;
5. strongest bear case.

Missing required process may be **blocked deterministically** once the relevant governing rules are approved and implemented.

## Drawdowns and ADD logic

Price declines alone are not sell signals.

A material drawdown triggers:

1. thesis reassessment;
2. valuation reassessment;
3. sizing / risk review;
4. decision.

First average-down requires thesis intact + valuation attractive + sizing permitted, with higher burden of proof than a normal add.

Second average-down requires **materially new or stronger evidence**.

Repeated average-downs require a progressively higher evidence burden.

Adding to winners is allowed when thesis remains intact, expected return remains attractive and sizing permits it.

## HOLD / TRIM / SELL / REVIEW

**HOLD** is the default when thesis, expected return and risk remain acceptable and no stronger condition exists.

**TRIM candidate** may surface for:

- excessive concentration;
- stretched valuation / materially reduced forward return;
- partial thesis deterioration.

A large gain alone is not a trim reason.

**SELL candidate** may surface when:

- thesis is fully invalidated; or
- forward expected return is clearly unattractive.

**REVIEW** is distinct from an action. It means **investigate first, decide second**.

Review triggers include material thesis / evidence / industry / management / balance-sheet / regulation changes, unusual price moves, passive concentration drift and stacked warning signs.

**INCONCLUSIVE is also distinct from REVIEW:** INCONCLUSIVE describes valuation evidence; REVIEW describes an investigation / decision state.

## BUY / ADD / HOLD / TRIM / SELL / REVIEW boundary

Calboard may eventually surface **candidates** under approved rules. It does not make the final qualitative investment judgement.

- BUY candidate: required process complete, quality + valuation bars clear, risks understood, invalidation + bear case present, sizing fits governing rules, speculative classification correct.
- ADD candidate: thesis intact, expected return attractive, sizing fits governing rules; falling-price adds require reassessment; repeated average-downs require stronger evidence.
- HOLD: default when ownership remains justified.
- TRIM candidate: concentration, stretched valuation or partial thesis deterioration.
- SELL candidate: thesis invalidated or expected return clearly unattractive.
- REVIEW: analysis required before action is known.

## AI role and comprehension

Default mode: **structured devil's advocate**.

For material decisions surface:

- strongest counterargument;
- missing evidence;
- assumption most capable of breaking the thesis.

Several risk flags may trigger deeper devil's-advocate review.

Objective hard-rule or required-process breach may be blocked **only where the governing rule is already approved**.

AI may research, synthesize, model, identify inconsistencies, challenge, enforce approved process and explain uncertainty.

AI may not invent new hard rules, silently choose unresolved policy, determine final thesis / management quality, claim precise intrinsic-value certainty, or make the final qualitative investment decision.

Deterministic software should own calculations, formulas, reconciliation, units, periods, financial identities and approved policy limits where reliable structured inputs exist. AI should primarily own qualitative interpretation, synthesis and challenge.

AI assistance should leave Calvin understanding the important reasoning well enough to challenge it. Faster synthesis that bypasses comprehension is not automatically better.

This comprehension principle is **not** a mandatory reading ritual or separate permanent gate.

## Stress discipline

For material or high-uncertainty investments, consider **joint stress**, not only one-variable sensitivity.

Ask what combination of operating weakness, funding pressure, dilution, timing slippage, balance-sheet stress or other relevant factors would break the thesis or materially change the valuation.

Use proportionately. No institutional stress engine is required unless simpler analysis proves insufficient.

## Decision learning

Where useful for consequential decisions, preserve dated expectations and later compare them with outcomes:

**prior expectation → new evidence / actual outcome → changed assumption → decision review → methodology learning.**

Judge not only whether an investment worked, but whether the reasoning was right for the right reason.

This is a methodology principle. Automated Research Memory, monitoring and backtesting infrastructure remain future capabilities unless separately approved.

## Minimum necessary complexity

Do **not** adopt complexity merely because institutions or AI practitioners use it.

Not governing requirements:

- mandatory ten-year quarterly models;
- source-count confidence scores;
- knowledge-graph infrastructure;
- AI-agent voting / consensus;
- autonomous investment decisions;
- automatic KPI monitoring;
- universal full three-statement models;
- universal APV / WACC machinery;
- institutional portfolio machinery without a demonstrated Calvin problem.

**Steal mechanisms, not stacks.**

Before adding complexity ask:

1. What problem does this solve?
2. Does Calvin actually have that problem?
3. Is the current methodology already solving it?
4. Is it financially sound?
5. Is there a simpler reliable method?
6. Is the extra complexity worth the maintenance and decision benefit?
